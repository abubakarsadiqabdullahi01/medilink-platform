# Phase 0 progress — what has been fixed, and what has not

*Written against System Blueprint v3.0 and Module Reference v3.0. Branch `claude/focused-thompson-q3c4g0`.*

The blueprint lists 119 P0 gaps, collected into fourteen pilot blockers. This note says which have been fixed in code, how each was shown to be fixed, and — as importantly — which have **not** been, and why.

**Where it stands: of the 119 P0 gaps, 87 are fixed, 18 are fixed in part and 14 are not done.** Every one of the fourteen that are not done needs something from outside engineering: the regulators' lists and figures, counsel, a signed money decision, an environment to deploy to. Of the fourteen blockers, one (facility onboarding) is closed in code. Several of the "fixed" rows still need something only a person outside engineering can supply (published terms, regulators' lists, counsel's sign-off); the caveat is on the row.

**What "fixed" means here.** Each fix was written test-first: the new test was run and seen to fail for the reason the gap predicts before the code changed, and where a test cannot see it (a screen, an interaction) it was driven in headless Chromium against a running server. A control case was run where a test could pass for the wrong reason. Nothing is counted as fixed because it was written.

**Verification.** 1,004 unit tests (70 files) and 1,117 integration tests (67 files, one complete serial run against a real PostgreSQL, no failures) pass; type-check and lint are clean. That run was made on the code as merged with `main` and committed with this note; before this work the suites held 600 unit and 563 integration tests. The demo seed was run to completion against a scratch database after the money and level changes and its state checked (every payout has an account, the cash account never goes negative, doctors have levels). The new screens were driven in headless Chromium against a dev server.

**Merged with `main`.** While this work was open, `main` gained its own fixes for AUT-07, AUT-10, NOT-01, NOT-03 to NOT-06, CRE-07, UXF-05 and DSP-03. Where the two overlapped, `main`'s version is kept: its text-delivery model (`notify` says whether a text went, a retry updates the failed row, a handler throws so the outbox retries), its handlers for offers, acceptances, document decisions, facility verification, hours and a dispute being opened, its staff-lookup gate, and its cross-tenant sweep and tests. What only this branch had was moved onto that design: severe-incident alerts, the later stages of a dispute (under review, decided, withdrawn), hours sent back, invoices, payments, payout accounts, expiry and approval reminders, shift reminders, the inbox. Two small additions ride on `main`'s handlers: an offer, an acceptance or a document query that happens a second time is told again, and a dispute opened by a facility tells the practitioner and tells the adjudicators. Two behaviours of this branch were given up: hours submitted now tell the facility's representative rather than everybody who can approve them, and a one-time code that does not go is no longer audited or consumed.

| CFG-02 | A Pay floors screen and route; every profession and shift type with no floor is listed. | **The figures.** `prisma/seed.ts` publishes placeholder floors for doctors and nurses only; every other profession's shifts are refused in a real deployment until somebody with `config.manage` sets one, and the demo seed publishes its own, labelled `demo-seed`. |
| BIL-02 | What can be paid out waits for the facility to pay (BIL-03), and the screens say so. | The ledger still credits a practitioner when an invoice is *raised*, which is the commercial question the blueprint asks, and "Owed to you" still means billed, not collected. |
| AUD-03 | Terms and privacy are enforced for a practitioner accepting an offer, a facility publishing, and anyone registering; a new version is asked for again. | A facility-level acceptance record (the hospital as an organisation, not the person) is not built, and the content of the documents is counsel's. |
| AUD-04 | `docs/data-inventory.md` (generated from the code and checked against the schema, so a new table cannot ship unclassified) lists every kind of data with purpose, proposed basis, owner, retention and what removes it. A daily job deletes one-time codes (7 days), ended sessions (30), the sign-in log (90), delivered queue entries (30) and abandoned uploads (7). | **Every period and every lawful basis is the team's proposal, not signed off by counsel or a data protection officer.** Messages, security events, search rankings, rejected documents and old availability have a proposed period that nothing enforces; money, disputes, incidents and the audit trail are kept with their legal period undecided. |
| BIL-07 | Recording a payment takes the bank's reference, the amount, the date it arrived and who paid; the amount must be the invoice's, the date cannot be in the future or before issue, and the audit row carries the amount and date but not the payer or reference. | Part payments, over-payments and one transfer covering several invoices have no model and are refused. |
| UXO-03 | A payout cannot be released by the person who prepared it (`PAYOUT_SECOND_APPROVER=off` for a pilot with one finance person, as a deliberate decision). | Recording a payment, and recording a transfer by hand, is still one person; payouts prepared before the preparer was recorded are not held back. |
| DSP-05 | A dispute holds the amount in dispute (BIL-10). | No credit note and no reversal entry for an upheld dispute; that waits on the money model. |

## Not done, and why

**All fourteen need something from outside engineering.**
- *The money model:* BIL-13 (the legal role and the signed Stage-3 decisions) and BIL-09, which follows the fee and credit-note decisions: an upheld dispute cannot change an invoice because there is no credit note, and what one costs, who bears the fee on it and whether a paid invoice is refunded are commercial questions. Whether Medilink may pay from its own funds is now a setting (`PAYOUT_REQUIRES_COLLECTION`); which it should be is a decision.
- *Counsel, a data protection officer, the regulator:* AUD-05.
- *The regulators' actual requirements:* CRE-01, CRE-02, CFG-01, CFG-03, UXP-03, QAL-06. The credential types, requirements and sanctions lists for nurses, pharmacists, laboratory scientists and radiographers, and the seed vocabularies the blueprint lists; inventing them would be a regulatory claim. The mechanisms to enter them (pay floors, levels, the sanctions list) exist.
- *A commercial statement:* UXW-06 (pricing). *A policy:* INC-02 (what an open incident restricts, and for whom).
- *An environment:* PLT-02, PLT-05, QAL-02 (a deploy pipeline and somewhere to deploy to, a restored backup, staging).

## Things to know when you pick this up

- **Fifteen new migrations** since the baseline: `timesheet_exact_bands`, `otp_send_budget`, `invitation_terms_accepted`, `assignment_rate_terms`, `invoice_lines_voidable`, `facility_registration_kind`, `credential_document_expiry`, `invoice_payment_record`, `payout_prepared_by`, `message_read_at`, `payout_manual_settlement`, `payout_accounts`, `professional_levels`, `registration_standing`, `request_levels`. Run `pnpm db:deploy` on any existing database, and restart anything holding the old Prisma client (a dev server keeps it in memory and fails with "Unknown argument" until restarted).
- **Run `pnpm db:seed` after deploying.** It registers the new scheduled jobs (`credential.expiry_warning_sweep`, `timesheet.approval_reminder_sweep`, `retention.purge_sweep`), the new permissions (`system.manage`, `data.erase`) and the holidays to 2030.
- **API contracts changed.** `register` accepts `acceptedTerms` (required once a privacy notice is published); `invoices/:id/pay` requires `paymentReference`, `amountKobo`, `paidOn` and `payerName`; `credentials/:id/review` accepts `printedExpiryDate`; `facilities/:id/verify` takes `checks` and evidence on approval; facilities carry `registrationKind`; `GET /payouts` carries `heldKobo`, `payableKobo`, `awaitingKobo` and `account`, and at platform scope `preparedByYou`, `secondApproverRequired` and `paidByHand`; a payout carries `paidOn`; `PayoutStatus` gains `CANCELLED`; creating or publishing a shift for a profession with no pay floor answers 422 `no_rate_floor`; preparing a payout can answer `nothing_collected`, `no_payout_account` or `payout_account_cooling_off`, and releasing `payout_account_changed`. New routes: `me/notifications` (+ `/read`), `rate-floors`, `payouts/:id/settle|fail|cancel`, `me/payout-account`, `payout-accounts`, `payout-accounts/practitioner/:id`, `payout-accounts/:id/review`; the dispute routes accept `facilityId`; `cadre-levels` (+ `/:id/active`), `registration-categories` (+ `/:id/active`) and `practitioners/verification/standing`. `POST /service-requests` accepts `cadreLevelId` and `supervisionAvailable`, and `shiftType` is optional: left out it is worked out from the hours, and one that disagrees with them is refused (422). `GET /reference` carries `cadreLevels` and `registrationCategories`; a registration carries its `cadreLevel` and `registrationCategory`; `POST /rate-floors` accepts `cadreLevelKey`. A request for a profession that has levels in use answers 422 `level_required` without one and `invalid_level` for one that is not that profession's; recording a level can answer `level_wrong_profession` or `registration_required`. The in-repo clients (`scripts/e2e.py`, `simulate.py`, `smoke-auth.ps1`, the Playwright specs, the demo seed) are updated; any other client is not. `verify-phone` now returns `setupToken` and `set-password` requires it.
- **New configuration.** `SUPPORT_PHONE`, `SUPPORT_WHATSAPP`, `SUPPORT_EMAIL`, `SUPPORT_HOURS` (shown on the public pages only when set); `PAYOUT_SECOND_APPROVER`, `PAYOUT_REQUIRES_COLLECTION` and `PAYOUT_REQUIRES_ACCOUNT` (leave unset; each is off only when set to exactly `off`, as a decision); `PAYOUT_ACCOUNT_COOLING_HOURS` (default 24; a bad value is read as 24, never 0); `PAYMENT_PROVIDER=manual` (pay by bank transfer and record it); **`PAYOUT_ACCOUNT_KEY` and `CLIENT_IP_HEADER`, which staging and production refuse to start without** (and `SMS_API_KEY` with `SMS_SENDER_ID` or `SMS_USERNAME` for a real SMS provider). Generate `PAYOUT_ACCOUNT_KEY` once (`openssl rand -base64 48`), keep it outside the database and its backups, and note that losing or changing it makes every stored account number unreadable, so practitioners are asked for theirs again. All are in `.env.example`.
- **New commands.** `scripts/create-first-administrator.ts` (in the production image) and `scripts/render-data-inventory.ts` (regenerate `docs/data-inventory.md` whenever the inventory changes; a test fails until you do).
- **For counsel and the data protection officer:** `docs/data-inventory.md` and the retention schedule in `src/modules/audit-compliance/domain/erasure.ts`. Neither has been reviewed.
- **The integration suite runs with `PAYOUT_SECOND_APPROVER`, `PAYOUT_REQUIRES_COLLECTION` and `PAYOUT_REQUIRES_ACCOUNT` off and the cooling-off at 0** so that the rest of the money flow can be driven by one person without paying every invoice or giving everybody an account first; the cases that are about each rule unset it.
- **Defining the first level for a profession is a switch.** From then every new request for it must name a level, and only practitioners whose level has been recorded against their registration are offered a shift that names one. Record levels for the people you want matched first (Practitioners, then Identity and registration): the Levels screen says, per profession, how many have none. A profession with no level behaves exactly as before.
- **Proposals taken in code, not signed off by anybody.** A shift is matched to one level exactly, and whether a higher level may take a lower shift is not decided. In a pay-floor lookup a floor for the request's level beats one for its specialty. Half or more of a shift between 20:00 and 06:00 WAT makes it a night shift, and a shift that straddles the line is held to the higher floor. A payout goes only to an account that has been the practitioner's for 24 hours. An account whose name shares fewer than two words with the registered name needs a written reason to verify. The three payout rules default on and are off only on the word `off`.
- **`.env` sets `NODE_ENV=development`**, which makes `next build` fail with an `<Html>` import error. Unset it for builds.
- **The demo seed** gives the suspended practitioner a shift at the flagship hospital, because a facility can only name someone it has booked, and releases its payouts as a second person. It also publishes demo pay floors for the professions the platform seed has none for, gives every practitioner a verified bank account (two have a replacement waiting, one in a relative's name), and revises the privacy notice last, because a revised notice has to be accepted before an offer can be. All of it is local-only and none of it is real.
- **Nothing here has been run on a phone, behind a real proxy, with a real SMS provider, or against a real bank.** The browser checks were headless Chromium against a dev server and a local object store (or a stub of one). The manual payout path has been exercised end to end in the tests and in a browser, never against an actual transfer.
